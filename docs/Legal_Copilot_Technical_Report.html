01 · Executive overview

What Legal Copilot does

Legal Copilot is a first-pass contract review workspace. A user uploads a document, watches a sequence of analysis agents, reviews risk observations anchored to exact contract wording, sees which legal sources were loaded, and receives a structured preliminary assessment with backend-generated verification labels.

Design principle: the system should make its evidence and uncertainty visible. A clause-level observation is not itself a finding of legal violation. If the source or jurisdiction is missing, the application should surface the gap instead of filling it with a guess.

Who it is for

The initial product hypothesis is a small in-house legal, procurement, or operations team that needs a traceable issue list before qualified counsel reviews the contract. This is a workflow hypothesis, not validated market demand or a claim that the product replaces lawyers.

What a successful review produces

  • Extracted and segmented document text with stable clause IDs.
  • Quote-grounded potential risks, including interactions across clauses where the evidence supports them.
  • Retrieved authority records and support-check outcomes, shown with explicit statuses.
  • A “Preliminary Contract Risk Assessment” with coverage gaps and mitigation suggestions.
  • DOCX/PDF export and a session purge action.
Important boundary: model quality, document quality, retrieved source coverage, jurisdiction, and provider availability can all limit results. This is a prototype for human review, not a legal opinion or a compliance certification.

03 · Processing pipeline

From upload to report

The graph is intentionally linear. Each node receives and updates a shared state object; progress is emitted as named events so the UI can show where the system is working or has failed.

#NodeWork performed
1ingestParse text already extracted by the ingestion service; if scanned content has no usable text, attempt multimodal Gemini extraction for supported PDF/image input. Preserve warnings where OCR/vision cannot read content.
2segmentCreate stable clause IDs, section/heading information, text spans/offsets, and a definitions map.
3detect_jurisdictionInspect contract language for governing law, venue, party locations, and data-subject regions. Record uncertainty rather than assume the applicable regime.
4checklist_scanScan clause batches for common issue families such as liability, indemnity, renewal, termination, data/security, IP, confidentiality, and governing law.
5cross_clause_scanGenerate candidate clause pairs using document-wide term overlap, defined-term references, topic links, and explicit section references; ask the model to assess genuine interactions and quote each involved clause.
6researchBuild queries from risk title/summary/evidence plus jurisdiction and subject hints. Retrieve relevant corpus chunks; optionally reorder candidates using configured embedding/reranking services. Search CourtListener where relevant.
7build_bankAssign stable authority IDs to resolved records and link risk observations to bank entries.
8draft_memoGenerate an executive summary in a Pydantic schema. Legal support claims are constructed by server code from loaded source sentences rather than free-form legal citations.
9verifyCheck clause quotes, authority IDs, source quotes, and support verdicts. Unsupported claims remain unverified or are removed according to the report rules.
10finalizeAssemble risk cards, loaded sources, “not assessed” regimes, evidence gaps, statuses, disclaimers, and exportable memo data.
Visible reasoning, safely framed: the UI exposes stage names and short progress summaries, not private chain-of-thought. The reviewable evidence is the quoted clause, its section, the selected authority, and the verifier’s result/reason.

State exchanged across nodes

The graph carries session and filename metadata; extracted document text and page texts; parsed clause records and definitions; jurisdiction findings; risks and warnings; retrieval keys/scores; authority candidates and bank entries; memo draft; verification results; progress events; and model-call/support-check metadata. The state is runtime data, not a persistent document database.

Segmentation and quote grounding

The segmenter assigns stable identifiers such as C1, C2, and so on, associates clauses with headings/sections, and tracks character offsets. Definitions are collected for use in cross-clause reasoning. Each proposed risk must point to existing clause IDs and include exact quote spans from those clauses. The report assembly rejects evidence when a quote cannot be found in the stated clause.

Cross-clause observations require multiple involved clause references and quotes aligned to each referenced clause. Candidate generation is generic: it uses shared terms, rare terms, explicit references, and definition relationships. The document’s wording is evidence; the model is asked to explain why an interaction may matter, not to declare a legal result.

Evidence gaps: redaction markers, omitted pages, truncation, unreadable scans, and absent attachments can limit review. The report lists detectable gaps and avoids implying that unseen material was assessed.

Upload and memory behavior

The API enforces supported media types, a configured request size limit (default 25 MB), and a session TTL (default one hour). PDF/DOCX/TXT parsing occurs in memory. The document bytes are cleared when analysis finishes, and session deletion removes the result and queued events. There is no persistent user-document storage in this implementation.

05 · Retrieval augmented generation

How RAG works here

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) supplies source material to a model or verifier at the time of a question. Legal Copilot’s legal-source retrieval combines a maintained local corpus with lexical ranking; optional provider integrations can add semantic ranking. Retrieval selects evidence candidates, but it does not prove that a provision applies or supports a proposition.

1. Source corpus and metadata

The local file backend/data/statutes.json stores entries with normalized keys and fields such as title, URL, source, kind, text, and retrieval timestamp. Topic/jurisdiction labels are maintained separately in statute_tags.json. The static resolver exposes only loaded article/section-level sources for research. Missing or empty text cannot be used as supporting authority.

2. Exact-source chunks

Loaded source entries are split into paragraph/subsection chunks by slicing the stored text. Chunk text is not paraphrased: each chunk remains an exact substring of its parent record and carries the parent key plus a locator. This allows the UI and support checker to stay scoped to the correct section.

3. Lexical BM25 ranking

BM25 ranks chunks by query-term relevance. The query combines the risk’s title, explanation, contract evidence and research questions with jurisdiction/subject hints. The implementation gates results using minimum score and distinct-term matches, applies topic/jurisdiction boosts or exclusions, caps chunks per source entry, and returns no source when no chunk passes the gate. This “empty retrieval” behavior is intentional: weak nearest matches should not be presented as authoritative.

4. Optional semantic ranking

If configured, Voyage voyage-law-2 creates query/document embeddings and an in-memory Qdrant collection; Cohere rerank-v3.5 can reorder BM25 candidates. These are optional external services. When absent or unavailable, the application falls back to BM25 instead of requiring a vector service.

5. Authority bank and generation

Resolved sources receive closed IDs such as [A1]. Claims may refer only to those IDs. The memo writer is instructed not to invent legal sources or cite free-form law; server-side logic builds support claims from eligible, loaded sentences. The result is then independently checked against the exact authority source text.

RAG is not legal validation: a high retrieval score means “relevant candidate,” not “governing law,” “correct interpretation,” or “the contract violates the law.” Applicability remains conditional on facts and jurisdiction.
06 · Citation integrity

Closed-set verification

  1. Extract and normalize. Deterministic patterns recognize supported case/statute citation forms and map them to normalized keys. Section/article identity matters; GDPR article citations resolve to article-scoped keys and do not fall back to the entire regulation.
  2. Resolve a real record. Static corpus lookups use exact normalized keys. CourtListener can resolve case citations and fetch opinion text where available. A search hit without opinion text is existence-only evidence, not support.
  3. Build the closed bank. Each resolved authority receives an ID, title, source, URL, retrieval metadata, text (if loaded), and status. A claim referencing an unknown ID is rejected.
  4. Ground the contract evidence. The claim’s clause quote must be found in the uploaded document. Risk report evidence must also match its cited clause.
  5. Run a separate support check. A checker call receives the claim and its cited authority text, separately from memo drafting. The model is asked for a supporting passage, and the checker response is validated against a strict Pydantic schema.
  6. Validate the quotation in code. The authority quote must appear in the cited text, be at least 60 characters, end in sentence punctuation, and not be heading/section-number-only. A “supports” verdict and grounded quote are both required for Verified.
  7. Render backend status. Report labels are computed from verifier results; the frontend renders returned labels and does not promote a status.

07 · Models and orchestration

Gemini calls, schemas, and resilience

Schema-constrained model calls

The shared Gemini wrapper requests JSON output with a schema generated from the Pydantic model, normalizes unsupported JSON Schema keywords, and validates the returned JSON locally. Analytical calls request temperature 0. The independent support-check model defaults to the configured primary model unless separately configured. Temperature 0 is a request parameter, not a guarantee of deterministic answers.

Configuration source of truth

backend/app/config.py loads environment variables and backend/.env relative to the config module, independent of the current working directory. Main keys include GEMINI_API_KEY, GEMINI_MODEL, optional GEMINI_FALLBACK_MODEL, SUPPORT_CHECK_MODEL, CourtListener credentials, and optional Voyage/Cohere keys. In hosted deployments, set secrets in the backend service environment and redeploy.

Context caching

The README describes Gemini provider-side implicit caching for repeated large prefixes as opportunistic. The application does not create explicit remote cache objects because a provider-side cache complicates purge guarantees. Cache hits and cost reductions depend on provider behavior and are not guaranteed; no fixed percentage savings should be claimed.

Why structured state matters: Pydantic models make each agent exchange predictable and testable. They do not eliminate model error; they limit the shape of the response and make failures explicit.
08 · Privacy, deployment, and API

Data lifecycle and operating model

Document lifecycle

  1. Browser holds the chosen file until upload.
  2. FastAPI creates a random session ID and stores file bytes in a process-local session object.
  3. Parsing, OCR, graph state, and results exist in runtime memory during the session.
  4. After analysis, original uploaded bytes are cleared; report state remains in memory to support viewing and export.
  5. Delete session clears result/error/event data and cancels any running task; TTL expiry also removes the session.
Privacy precision: the app does not write user documents to persistent local storage, but it is not “zero knowledge” and cannot promise provider-side zero retention. Gemini, CourtListener, Voyage, and Cohere may receive processing inputs when their corresponding functions are used. Review account settings, contracts, and data terms before uploading sensitive documents.

Current hosting pattern

Recommended prototype layout: Next.js frontend on Vercel and FastAPI backend on Render. The frontend’s NEXT_PUBLIC_API_URL must point to the backend origin or use the configured same-origin proxy route. Keep Gemini, CourtListener, Voyage, and Cohere secrets only in Render’s backend environment. Configure CORS, HTTPS, request limits, and access-log redaction.

Deployment constraint: sessions are process-local. Use one backend worker/instance for the prototype. Multiple workers or replicas can make a session created on one process unavailable on another; a privacy-reviewed ephemeral session coordinator is required before scaling horizontally.

09 · User experience and errors

What a reviewer sees

1 · Upload

Choose PDF, DOCX, TXT, or supported image. File size/type and provider-handling notices appear before analysis.

2 · Agent trace

Ten workflow stages show waiting/running/completed/failed progress from backend events.

3 · Clause evidence

Each risk links to its section and exact quoted text, including each clause for cross-clause findings.

4 · Report and authorities

Risk cards include conditional impact, action, source IDs, support quote, and backend status. The report surfaces evidence/source gaps.

5 · Citation probe

A pasted citation can test whether a source record resolves. The interface explains that record lookup is not proposition verification.

6 · Export / purge

Download a memo as DOCX/PDF, then delete the session to purge in-memory results.

Failure states are part of the product

Unavailable model keys, model-not-found errors, provider overload/rate limits, network errors, expired sessions, missing source text, failed OCR, and API downtime should be shown as actionable states. The UI should not substitute sample data or silently upgrade a citation badge. The backend error response is designed to include a category and sanitized diagnostics without disclosing credentials.

Accessibility and professional review

  • Use text labels alongside colors for status badges.
  • Preserve exact evidence text; do not normalize the displayed quote.
  • Keep “not legal advice” and “preliminary assessment” visible.
  • Provide clear upload, retry, export, and purge actions.
  • Do not expose private model chain-of-thought; expose stage, evidence, sources, and reasons.

Benchmark qualification: the latest memo-generation change has not been measured by a fresh live evaluation. Older eval numbers in the repository predate that change and are not presented here as current performance.

Recommended evaluation protocol

  1. Use a counsel-reviewed, jurisdiction-labeled contract set with planted and naturally occurring issues.
  2. Score exact quote grounding, section match, non-obvious risk recall, cross-clause recall, false positive rate, source identity precision, and support-check success separately.
  3. Report abstention and “not assessed” coverage, not only positive detections.
  4. Measure time to reviewer-ready issue list against a baseline workflow, with blind reviewer scoring.
  5. Repeat across document types (native PDF, scanned PDF, DOCX, image), page counts, and extraction quality.
  6. Track latency, provider error rates, cost, retries, and cache behavior with document content excluded from logs.

What the current tests do not establish

  • They do not prove legal correctness or guarantee recall on arbitrary contracts.
  • They do not replace a live, independently labeled evaluation of the current model/prompt configuration.
  • They do not establish provider-side retention behavior, production availability, or multi-worker session safety.
11 · Constraints and roadmap

Known limitations and next steps

Current limitations

  • Source coverage is finite. A static corpus entry supports only its own section/article and loaded text. Missing jurisdictions/regimes must remain “not assessed.”
  • Support is conditional. A verifier status reflects a constrained evidence check; it does not resolve choice-of-law questions or provide a final legal interpretation.
  • OCR and vision can fail. Poor scans, stamps, handwriting, tables, and low contrast may degrade extraction.
  • External services are dependencies. The model, citation search, embeddings, and reranker depend on network access, keys, quotas, and provider availability.
  • Session memory is single-process. Restarts lose sessions and multiple workers are not coordinated.
  • No quantified cost/cache claim. Implicit context caching is provider-controlled; no fixed savings are guaranteed.
